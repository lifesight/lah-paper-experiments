# Benchmarks and evaluation methodology for LLM agents (positioning a production marketing-measurement agent harness)

Retrieval date for every leaderboard snapshot below: 2026-11-05 unless a different date is stated. "Paper claims" and "leaderboard shows today" are kept separate in each entry. Anything marked UNVERIFIED could not be confirmed against the owning source in this session.

## Key question 1: General agent benchmarks (metric, what the harness controls, headline numbers with dates)

### Takeaway
Every widely cited agent benchmark except BFCL scores a (harness, model) pair, not a model: the leaderboards of SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 2.0, HAL, OSWorld-Verified, Spider 2.0 and DABstep all list an agent/scaffold column, and only SWE-bench's "Bash Only" track, BFCL and (by convention) OSWorld's general-model rows hold the harness fixed. Headline numbers have saturated on SWE-bench Verified (about 96-97 percent on third-party mirrors by 2026-09) and are in the 80s on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and OSWorld-Verified, so a 2026 paper should not position against raw scores but against the protocol (trials, CIs, cost, harness disclosure).

### Inferences
- The benchmarks a measurement-agent paper would be compared against are either system leaderboards (harness x model, with scaffold named per row) or model leaderboards under a locked harness (BFCL, SWE-bench Bash Only, Epoch). A paper about a harness belongs to the first group and must name both the harness and the model per row, with the model held fixed across harness variants.
- CI reporting is now expected on the better-run leaderboards (Terminal-Bench 2.0 prints +/-95% CI; SWE-bench Pro prints +/-3.60; OSWorld prints Avg +/- Std only when Runs > 1). OSWorld's many "Runs: 1" rows are the counter-example to avoid.
- tau-bench's database-state check is the closest structural precedent to a measurement agent: the correct outcome is a verifiable system state (a saved plan, a promoted allocation, a written figure), not free text.

## Key question 2: Evidence that the harness or scaffold, not only the model, drives results

### Takeaway
Same-model, different-harness spreads of 10-25 percentage points are documented on every major agent benchmark; the strongest controlled evidence is the May 2026 factorial study in which harness variance averaged 7.8x model variance and reversed six of nine model rankings, and the Terminal-Bench 2.0 leaderboard itself (Claude Opus 4.6: 58.0% under Claude Code to 76.4% under Meta-Harness).

### Cited Findings
- "Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness" (Zhang, Wang, Ge, Xu, Hamm, Reddy; submitted 2026-05-07): on a 100-task SWE-bench Verified subset, moving from a minimal harness (H1) to a full harness (H3) changed GLM-5.1 52.5% to 65.5% (+13.0 pp), GPT-5.4 55.0% to 63.5% (+8.5 pp), Kimi K2.6 52.0% to 60.5% (+8.5 pp); "harness variance averaged 7.80x model variance, with six model-pair ranking reversals across nine comparisons"; the authors "do not claim this ratio is universal" - [arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950)
- Same paper's survey table: SWE-bench Verified scaffold-only variation up to 11 pp (GPT-5) and 15 pp (Kimi K2 Thinking) citing Epoch AI; Grok 4 58.6% to 72-75% from SWE-agent to xAI's scaffold; SWE-bench Verified Mini Claude Sonnet 4.5 68% (SWE-Agent) vs 34% (HAL Generalist), GPT-5 Medium 46% vs 12%, o4-mini nearly 48 pp, citing HAL; SWE-bench Pro Claude Opus 4.5 45.9% (SEAL) to 55.4% (Claude Code); Terminal-Bench 2 GPT-5.4 (high) 69.7% to 77.0% from harness evolution alone (AHE); a fixed model 52.8% to 66.5% from prompt, middleware and verification changes; Vercel: 15 tools to 2 tools raised an agent task from 80% to 100% - [arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950)
- Its prescription: a "Harness Card" covering Execution, Tool, Context, Scheduling, Observability, Verification, Governance; either a locked-harness protocol (one harness for all models) or a factorial protocol reporting variance components; report harness variance alongside model variance plus trajectory-level metrics (recovery rate, context retention, control lag) - [arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950)

### Inferences
- The field now has a named failure mode (undisclosed harness) and a named remedy (Harness Card, locked-harness or factorial protocol). A harness paper that reports a model-fixed, harness-varied factorial with per-cell SD over >=3 runs would meet the 2026 standard set by Zhang et al. and Harness2.
- The Terminal-Bench 2.0 rows show the ordering of harnesses is model-dependent (Droid is second for Opus 4.5 but seventh for Opus 4.6), which is exactly the ranking-reversal finding of Zhang et al.; a paper should therefore test its harness on at least two model families.
- Harness2's within-cell SDs (0.9-4.5 points) are of the same order as several of its claimed k=1 gains on JobBench (1.4-4.5 points), so three runs is a floor, not a comfortable number, for gains under 5 points.

### Gaps
- No Anthropic or OpenAI first-party post isolating scaffold effects with numbers was retrieved beyond the 2024 SWE-bench Verified launch post (403) and the CORE-Bench grading anecdote.
- Epoch AI's scaffold-by-scaffold table was not read directly.

## Key question 3: Reporting standards for agent results (trials, pass^k, cost per task, tokens, latency, variance)

### Takeaway
The 2024-2026 literature converges on: multiple independent runs per task with CIs or SD; pass@k as the optimistic bound and pass^k as the pessimistic bound; dollar cost and tokens per task on the same chart as accuracy (Pareto frontier); clean environments per trial; mandatory transcript inspection; and reliability metrics (ICC) alongside accuracy, because single-run pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified moves by 2.2-6.0 points depending on which run is picked.

### Cited Findings
- Kapoor et al., "AI Agents That Matter" (2024): accuracy-only evaluation produces "needlessly complex and costly agents" and "mistaken conclusions about the sources of accuracy gains"; recommends jointly optimizing cost and accuracy, separating benchmarks for model developers from downstream developers, proper holdouts (only 1 of 8 domain-general and 0 of 2 fully general benchmarks had appropriate holdouts across 17 surveyed), and standardized evaluation; joint optimization cut HotPotQA variable cost 53% (GPT-3.5) and 41% (Llama-3-70B) at similar accuracy; evaluating full SWE-bench "could cost over USD 8,000 for a single evaluation run"; many reported HumanEval/WebArena accuracies exceeded the maximum of five reproduction runs - [arXiv 2407.01502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502)
- HAL (Kapoor and 30 co-authors, submitted 2025-10-13, ICLR 2026): "21,730 agent rollouts across 9 models and 9 benchmarks ... total cost of about $40,000", 2.5B tokens of logs shared; higher reasoning effort gave equal or lower accuracy in 21 of 36 model-scaffold-benchmark combinations; the most expensive model is on the cost-accuracy Pareto frontier in only 1 of 9 benchmarks; average cost per evaluation ranges from about $13 (ScienceAgentBench) to over $450 (Online Mind2Web); Opus 4.1 was skipped on Online Mind2Web at an estimated $20,000 - [arXiv 2510.11977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977)
- HAL log analysis (Docent, 1,634 transcripts, 36 model-scaffold pairs): eight cases of agents finding gold answers on HuggingFace or arXiv; agents hard-coding solutions to pass unit tests; an agent using an incorrect credit card in a flight booking; instruction violations in over 60% of failed AssistantBench/CORE-Bench tasks; environmental barriers in about 40% of failed tasks; self-correction made success 1.5x to 4x more likely; explicit verification steps made success 13% to 87% more likely; a data-leakage bug in the TAU-bench Few Shot agent invalidated its results - [arXiv 2510.11977 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977)
- HAL site (read 2026-11-05): per entry it shows agent, model (with release month), accuracy and cost in dollars; "Agents can be 100x more expensive while only being 1% better"; "We have paused updating HAL leaderboard with new models and are currently focusing on measuring reliability"; 26,597 rollouts; nine benchmarks: AssistantBench, CORE-Bench Hard, GAIA, Online Mind2Web, Scicode, ScienceAgentBench, SWE-bench Verified Mini, TAU-bench Airline, USACO; the paper acknowledges incomplete cost accounting for caching with SWE-Agent (secondary) - [hal.cs.princeton.edu](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/), [huggingface.co/blog/evaleval](https://huggingface.co/blog/evaleval/eval-costs-bottleneck)
- tau-bench pass^k: introduced "to evaluate the reliability of agent behavior over multiple trials"; gpt-4o "pass^8 <25% in retail" - [arXiv 2406.12045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)
- Anthropic, "Demystifying evals for AI agents" (published 2026-01-09 per secondary summaries): "pass@k measures the likelihood that an agent gets at least one correct solution in k attempts"; "pass^k measures the probability that all k trials succeed"; a 75% per-trial rate gives about 42% pass^3; "Because model outputs vary between runs, we run multiple trials"; each trial from a clean environment (Claude once examined git history left by previous trials); start with 20-50 tasks drawn from real failures; capability evals start low, regression evals sit near 100%; prefer code graders, use LLM graders where necessary, humans sparingly; grade outcomes not paths; partial credit for multi-part tasks; "Read the transcripts!"; a 0% pass rate even at pass@100 usually means a broken task; track token usage, latency, cost per task and error rates on a static task bank - [anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- Claude Code plugin evals (official docs): "One run of a non-deterministic agent tells you little, so each case runs three times by default"; run score = fraction of graders passed (weighted); case score = mean across runs; a case passes at `--threshold` (default 1.0); every case also runs a no-plugin baseline arm and reports WITH, W/OUT and delta; six grader types (`regex`, `tool_used`, `tool_order`, `file_exists` computed from transcript and files at no cost; `llm` and `baseline` call a judge, "three short judge calls per llm or baseline grader per run"); `--judge-model` selects the judge; `--runs <n>`, `--max-cost-usd`, `--json` result document; graders that cannot pass without the plugin (e.g. `tool_used: Skill`) are excluded from both arms so they do not inflate delta; each run starts in an empty isolated workspace; requires Claude Code v2.1.269+ - [code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-evals](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-evals)
- "Stochasticity in Agentic Evaluations: Quantifying Inconsistency with Intraclass Correlation" (submitted 2025-12-07): ICC on GAIA ranged 0.304-0.774 across models and 0.4955-0.7118 on FRAMES; ICC converges by 8-16 trials for structured tasks and 32+ for complex reasoning; "We recommend reporting accuracy alongside ICC and within-query variance as standard practice" with updated Evaluation Cards - [arXiv 2512.06710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06710)
- "On Randomness in Agentic Evals" (Bjarnason, Silva, Monperrus; v1 2026-02-06, v3 2026-03-25): 60,000 SWE-bench Verified trajectories over three models and two scaffolds; "single-run pass@1 estimates vary by 2.2 to 6.0 percentage points depending on which run is selected"; SD exceeds 1.5 pp even at temperature 0; trajectories diverge within the first few percent of tokens; recommends multiple independent runs, statistical power analysis to size the run count, and reporting pass@k (optimistic) with pass^k (pessimistic) for k>1 - [arXiv 2602.07150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07150)
- Terminal-Bench 2.0 prints "+/- 95% confidence interval" on every row; SWE-bench Pro prints +/-3.60 on 730 problems; OSWorld shows Avg +/- Std only for multi-run rows and lists "Runs: 1" for most entries; Harness2 reports mean and SD over three runs with shuffled task order - [tbench.ai](https://www.tbench.ai/?version=2.0), [scaleapi.github.io](https://scaleapi.github.io/SWE-bench_Pro-os), [osworld-v1.xlang.ai](https://osworld-v1.xlang.ai/), [arXiv 2610.14224](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14224)
- BFCL reports benchmark-wide cost in USD and per-call latency (seconds) next to accuracy - [gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)
- DeepSWE (2026, secondary snippet): with only about four runs a configuration whose runs land close together looks precise even when true uncertainty is large; methods accounting for task selection give wider intervals - [arXiv 2607.07946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07946)
- Practitioner guidance (vendor blog): at least three seeds per configuration, report variance with the mean, bootstrap 95% CIs on aggregates - [mlflow.org](https://mlflow.org/articles/tags/how-to-benchmark-ai-agents/)
- Hugging Face EvalEval commentary: scaffold choice creates a 33x cost spread that 1-D leaderboards hide (secondary) - [huggingface.co/blog/evaleval](https://huggingface.co/blog/evaleval/eval-costs-bottleneck)

### Inferences
- A defensible minimum protocol for a harness paper in late 2026: >=3 runs per task per cell (5 if gains under 5 points are claimed), per-cell mean and SD or bootstrap CI, pass@k and pass^k for k in {1,3,5}, ICC on the per-task outcome, dollar cost and input/output/cached tokens per task, wall-clock latency per task, a no-harness (bare model, minimal tool loop) control arm, and a published transcript sample with a log-inspection table of failure classes.
- The plugin-evals "with vs without" delta is a ready-made template for a production harness: the same tasks run with the full harness and with a stripped harness, and the delta is the harness's contribution.

### Gaps
- No single consensus run count exists; sources recommend 3 (plugin evals, MLflow), 5 (Terminal-Bench submissions, secondary), 8-32 (ICC convergence) or power analysis.
- HAL's per-task runs and variance were not stated in the portion of the paper read.

## Key question 4: LLM-as-judge reliability and mitigations

### Takeaway
GPT-4-class judges agree with humans at about the human-human rate (over 80% on MT-Bench) but carry position, verbosity and self-preference biases, with self-preference causally linked to self-recognition; the standard mitigations across the primary sources and Anthropic's guidance are a separate judge model, per-criterion isolated rubric graders, an explicit "Unknown" escape, calibration against human labels, and deterministic graders wherever the output is checkable.

### Cited Findings
- Zheng et al., "Judging LLM-as-a-judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" (v1 2023-06-09, v4 2023-12-24): GPT-4 judges reach "over 80% agreement" with human preferences, "the same level of agreement between humans"; identified position bias, verbosity bias, self-enhancement bias and limited reasoning ability; the abstract says the authors "propose solutions to mitigate some of them" (the specific mitigations such as position swapping and reference-guided judging are in the paper body, not confirmed from the abstract fetch) - [arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)
- Panickssery, Bowman and Feng, "LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations" (2024-04-15): GPT-4 and Llama 2 distinguish their own outputs from other LLMs' and from human text with better-than-chance accuracy out of the box; after fine-tuning, "a linear correlation between self-recognition capability and the strength of self-preference bias"; controlled experiments indicate the link is "not easily explained by simple confounding factors" - [arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)
- Shi et al., "Judging the Judges: A Systematic Study of Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" (IJCNLP-AACL 2025): 15 judges across MTBench and DevBench, over 150,000 evaluation instances; introduces Position Consistency, Preference Fairness and Repetition Stability; position bias is "weakly influenced by the length of prompt components, but strongly affected by the quality gap between solutions", is not random chance, and "varies significantly across judges and tasks" - [arXiv 2406.07791](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v9)
- Survey "A Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge" (2024): self-enhancement bias means evaluators "tend to favor their own outputs"; recommends avoiding the same model as generator and judge - [arXiv 2411.15594](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15594); "LLMs-as-Judges: A Comprehensive Survey" classifies position bias as presentation-related and self-enhancement as a separate category - [arXiv 2412.05579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05579)
- Anthropic guidance: calibrate model graders "closely against human experts"; give the judge an escape option such as returning "Unknown" to reduce hallucinated grades; "use structured rubrics and grade each dimension with its own isolated judge"; prefer deterministic graders; check whether a failure is "a real agent mistake or a grader rejecting a valid solution" - [anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- Claude Code plugin evals: `llm` graders are non-deterministic and "differ more the longer the text", so long outputs should be graded with `regex` over the file and `llm` kept for short outputs "with rubrics written as concrete PASS and FAIL conditions"; each `llm` grader takes three judge votes per run; "If a case's tool_used: Skill grader passes but delta is negative, suspect the judge before the plugin. A small judge model can mark a correct answer wrong because it's formatted differently"; the judge model is selectable with `--judge-model` - [code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-evals](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-evals)
- Judge disclosure in practice: Harness2 names its judges (JobBench weighted rubric judged by Claude Opus 4.8; LAB per-criterion by Claude Sonnet 4.6) and uses Claude Opus 4.8 as the harness editor, so judge and editor share a family - [arXiv 2610.14224 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14224)
- A 2026 bias-mitigation paper uses models from four families specifically to measure self-preference bias - [arXiv 2604.23178](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23178)

### Inferences
- For a measurement agent most graded quantities (figures, allocations, deltas, significance flags) are checkable against the system of record, so reference-based deterministic graders can carry the primary score; an LLM judge should be confined to narrative quality (caveats stated, no invented figures) with per-criterion rubrics, a judge from a different model family than the executor, and a reported human-agreement rate on a labelled sample.
- Reference-free judging of numeric answers is the riskiest configuration given the documented "limited reasoning ability" of judges; reference-based comparison with tolerance is the defensible choice.

### Gaps
- Zheng et al.'s concrete mitigation list (swap positions, few-shot, reference-guided, chain-of-thought) was not confirmed from the fetched abstract.
- No 2025-2026 primary source was found giving human-agreement rates for LLM judges on agent transcripts specifically (as opposed to chat responses).

- A measurement-agent paper can truthfully claim there is no prior benchmark for MMM, attribution reconciliation, budget optimisation or geo-experiment readout agents, and should position AD-Bench (advertising analytics, production requests, replayed expert trajectories, Pass@k plus trajectory coverage) as the nearest prior work and DABstep plus Spider 2.0 as the nearest numeric precedents.

## Key question 7 (synthesis): what a production measurement-agent harness paper should position against and propose

### Takeaway
Position against the harness-disclosure literature (Zhang et al. 2026, Harness2, HAL) and the nearest numeric benchmarks (DABstep, Spider 2.0, AD-Bench), not against saturated coding leaderboards; propose a locked-model factorial protocol with deterministic reference-based grading from the system of record, multi-run reliability metrics, and cost/latency per task.

### Cited Findings
- All findings in Key questions 1-6 above (no new sources in this section).

### Inferences
- Positioning: (a) the benchmark gap of Key question 6; (b) the harness-effect evidence of Key question 2 as the motivation for evaluating the harness as the unit; (c) tau-bench's state-based grading and DABstep's typed exact match as grader precedents; (d) AD-Bench's trajectory coverage as the precedent for scoring tool-path fidelity when figures must come from recorded tool results rather than model arithmetic.
- Protocol to propose, each element traceable to a cited source: Harness Card for the harness (Zhang et al.); model fixed, harness varied, with a bare-tool-loop control arm (Zhang et al.; plugin-evals delta); >=3 runs per task per cell, mean and SD or bootstrap CI, pass@k and pass^k for k=1,3,5 (Anthropic; Bjarnason et al.; tau-bench); ICC on per-task outcomes (arXiv 2512.06710); deterministic graders for figures and end-state, LLM judge from a different family only for narrative criteria with per-criterion rubrics and a human-agreement rate (Anthropic; Panickssery; Zheng); gold answers generated by replaying tool calls against the platform, with an annotation-audit step (AD-Bench; Jin et al.); dollar cost, tokens (input/output/cached) and latency per task plotted as a Pareto frontier (Kapoor et al.; HAL; BFCL); an AgentDojo-style injected-content suite with benign utility, utility under attack and targeted ASR, since a measurement agent reads documents and tool outputs; a transcript-inspection table classifying failures (HAL Docent categories: shortcuts, instruction violations, environmental barriers, tool errors, self-correction, verification).
