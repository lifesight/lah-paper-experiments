## Key question 1: confirm the candidate list, drop what cannot be verified

### Takeaway
All candidates except one survey could be verified against a primary page. Two candidate attributions were wrong and are corrected below: the "accuracy falls as tool count grows" result lives in MCP-Zero's Table 1 (and, qualitatively, in RAG-MCP's stress test), and the arXiv id secondary pages give for the Meng et al. harness survey (2605.29682) belongs to a different paper.

### Cited Findings

#### Agent loops and tool use

- **ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models.** Shunyu Yao et al. 2022 (ICLR 2023 camera-ready is v3). arXiv:2210.03629. https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629 . Verified: arXiv (v3, 10 Mar 2023; comments say v3 is the ICLR camera-ready). Claim: interleaving reasoning traces with actions lets the model plan and fetch external information; +34% absolute success on ALFWorld and +10% on WebShop over imitation/RL baselines with one or two in-context examples; on HotpotQA/FEVER, querying a Wikipedia API reduces the hallucination and error propagation seen with chain-of-thought.
- **Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools.** Timo Schick et al. 2023. arXiv:2302.04761; NeurIPS 36 (2023) pp. 68539-68551, DOI 10.52202/075280-2997. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 9 Feb 2023) and OpenAlex record for the NeurIPS version (proceedings page itself not opened). Claim: a LM trained self-supervised from a handful of demonstrations per API learns which API to call, when, with what arguments, and how to use the result (calculator, QA, two search engines, translation, calendar); no numeric result in the abstract. Note: the NeurIPS author list adds Eric Hambro (seen only in a proceedings.com search listing).
- **Executable Code Actions Elicit Better LLM Agents (CodeAct).** Xingyao Wang et al. 2024. arXiv:2402.01030; ICML 2024 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01030 . Verified: arXiv (v4, 7 Jun 2024). Claim: executable Python as a unified action space gives up to 20% higher success rate than JSON/text actions across 17 LLMs on API-Bank and a newly curated benchmark; CodeActInstruct has 7k multi-turn interactions.
- **SWE-agent: Agent-Computer Interfaces Enable Automated Software Engineering.** John Yang et al. 2024. arXiv:2405.15793. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793 . Verified: arXiv (v3, 11 Nov 2024). Claim: the agent-computer interface (ACI) is a design object in its own right; 12.5% pass@1 on SWE-bench and 87.7% on HumanEvalFix.
- **OpenHands: An Open Platform for AI Software Developers as Generalist Agents** (formerly OpenDevin). Xingyao Wang et al. 2024. arXiv:2407.16741; ICLR 2025 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16741 . Verified: arXiv (v3, 18 Apr 2025). Claim: open platform with sandboxed code execution, multi-agent coordination and integrated evaluation on 15 benchmarks including SWE-bench and WebArena; 2.1K+ contributions from 188+ contributors.
- **Gorilla: Large Language Model Connected with Massive APIs.** Shishir G. Patil et al. 2023. arXiv:2305.15334. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15334 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 24 May 2023). Claim: even GPT-4 often generates wrong input arguments and hallucinates API usage; retriever-aware fine-tuning lets the model track documentation changes at test time and reduces hallucination; introduces APIBench. No numbers in the abstract.
- **The Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL): From Tool Use to Agentic Evaluation of Large Language Models.** Shishir G. Patil et al. 2025. ICML 2025, PMLR 267:48371-48392. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/patil25a.html . Verified: PMLR page. Claim: AST-based evaluation of serial/parallel function calls across languages that scales to thousands of functions; also tests abstention and multi-step stateful agentic use; finding: state-of-the-art models excel at single-turn calls while memory, dynamic decision-making and long-horizon reasoning remain open. No arXiv id found.
- **MCP-Zero: Active Tool Discovery for Autonomous LLM Agents.** Xiang Fei, Xiawu Zheng, Hao Feng. 2025. arXiv:2506.01056. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01056 (numbers from https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01056v4 Table 1, Sec. 5.3). Verified: arXiv (v4, 24 Jun 2025). Claim (the tool-count result): with standard schema injection on APIBank, Claude-3.5 accuracy falls from 97.60% to 69.23% (single-turn) and from 100.00% to 60.22% (multi-turn) when the candidate pool grows from the domain subset to the full pool (about 40x more APIs, 6.3k-6.4k tokens of schemas), while MCP-Zero's on-demand retrieval holds 95.19% / 90.32% at 111-159 tokens (98% token reduction). Caveat: GPT-4.1 (98.08 to 94.71) and Gemini-2.5 (92.79 to 94.23) show little or no drop in the same table, so the degradation is model-dependent; cite it as such. The abstract itself contains no accuracy-vs-tool-count figure.
- **RAG-MCP: Mitigating Prompt Bloat in LLM Tool Selection via Retrieval-Augmented Generation.** Tiantian Gan, Qiyao Sun. 2025. arXiv:2505.03275. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03275 (stress test from https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03275v1 Sec. 4.1/5, Fig. 3). Verified: arXiv (v1, 6 May 2025). Claim: with the full MCP catalog in the prompt, tool-selection accuracy is 13.62% (2,133.84 prompt tokens); retrieving candidates first gives 43.13% (1,084 tokens, about 49% fewer; the abstract says "over 50%"). Stress test is qualitative only: success above 90% when fewer than about 30 tools are offered, intermittent failures at 31-70, failures dominate beyond about 100 (heat map, no numeric axis). This is the second primary source for "more tools, worse selection".

#### Agent benchmarks, pass^k, cost and latency reporting

- **tau-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains.** Shunyu Yao, Noah Shinn, Pedram Razavi, Karthik Narasimhan. 2024. arXiv:2406.12045. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 17 Jun 2024). Claim: defines the pass^k reliability metric (success on all k i.i.d. trials of a task); state-of-the-art function-calling agents such as gpt-4o succeed on fewer than 50% of tasks, and pass^8 is below 25% in the retail domain; correctness is judged by comparing final database state with an annotated goal state.
- **tau^2-Bench: Evaluating Conversational Agents in a Dual-Control Environment.** Victor Barres et al. 2025. arXiv:2506.07982. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07982 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 9 Jun 2025). Claim: Telecom dual-control domain modelled as a Dec-POMDP where the simulated user also acts on shared state; ablations separate reasoning errors from communication/coordination errors; performance drops significantly from no-user to dual-control (no number in the abstract).
- **AgentBench: Evaluating LLMs as Agents.** Xiao Liu et al. 2023. arXiv:2308.03688; ICLR 2024 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03688 . Verified: arXiv (v3, 4 Oct 2025). Claim: 8 environments; the main causes of failure are poor long-term reasoning, decision-making and instruction following; the abstract's model count renders as an unexpanded LaTeX macro on the page.
- **GAIA: a benchmark for General AI Assistants.** Gregoire Mialon et al. 2023. arXiv:2311.12983. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12983 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 21 Nov 2023). Claim: 466 real-world questions (300 held for the leaderboard) needing reasoning, multi-modality, browsing and tool use; humans 92% vs GPT-4 with plugins 15%.
- **Terminal-Bench: Benchmarking Agents on Hard, Realistic Tasks in Command Line Interfaces.** Mike A. Merrill et al. (85 authors). 2026. arXiv:2601.11868; ICLR 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868 and https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/444a3737adaee10d86ad2ef5f74468e6-Abstract-Conference.html . Verified: arXiv (v1, 17 Jan 2026) and ICLR 2026 proceedings page. Claim: Terminal-Bench 2.0 has 89 containerised terminal tasks, each with a human-written solution and tests; frontier models and agents resolve fewer than 65%; ships an evaluation harness. Note: the paper is dated 2026, not 2025; the benchmark site predates it.
- **AI Agents That Matter.** Sayash Kapoor, Benedikt Stroebl, Zachary S. Siegel, Nitya Nadgir, Arvind Narayanan. 2024. arXiv:2407.01502. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 1 Jul 2024). Claim: agent benchmarks report accuracy without cost, which rewards needlessly complex and expensive agents; argues for joint cost-accuracy optimisation, proper holdout sets to stop shortcut overfitting, and standardised evaluation for reproducibility. No numbers in the abstract.
- **Holistic Agent Leaderboard: The Missing Infrastructure for AI Agent Evaluation.** Sayash Kapoor et al. (31 authors). 2025. arXiv:2510.11977; ICLR 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977 and https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/a0928f924a344aaebbb7f6cd8d56e34c-Abstract-Conference.html . Verified: arXiv (v1, 13 Oct 2025) and ICLR 2026 proceedings page. Claim: standardised evaluation harness over hundreds of VMs; 21,730 rollouts across 9 models and 9 benchmarks for about $40,000; higher reasoning effort reduced accuracy in the majority of runs; LLM-aided log inspection found agents searching HuggingFace for benchmark answers and misusing credit cards; 2.5B tokens of logs released.
- **DABstep: Data Agent Benchmark for Multi-step Reasoning.** Alex Egg et al. 2025. arXiv:2506.23719. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23719 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 30 Jun 2025). Claim: 450+ tasks from a financial analytics platform combining code-based data processing with reasoning over documentation, factoid answers checked automatically; best agent reaches 14.55% on the hardest tasks.

#### LLM-as-judge reliability

- **Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena.** Lianmin Zheng et al. 2023. arXiv:2306.05685; NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685 . Verified: arXiv (v4, 24 Dec 2023). Claim: strong judges such as GPT-4 reach over 80% agreement with human preferences, the same level as human-human agreement; documents position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases and limited reasoning ability of judges; releases 3K expert votes and 30K conversations.

#### Grounding and hallucination of numeric figures

- **FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation.** Sewon Min et al. 2023. arXiv:2305.14251; EMNLP 2023 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 11 Oct 2023). Claim: decompose a generation into atomic facts and score the fraction supported by a knowledge source; ChatGPT scores 58% on biographies; the automated estimator has under 2% error; scoring 6,500 generations from 13 LMs would have cost $26K by hand.
- **Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations (ALCE).** Tianyu Gao, Howard Yen, Jiatong Yu, Danqi Chen. 2023. arXiv:2305.14627; EMNLP 2023 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 31 Oct 2023). Claim: benchmark with automatic metrics for fluency, correctness and citation quality; on ELI5 even the best systems lack complete citation support 50% of the time.
- **A Survey on Hallucination in Large Language Models: Principles, Taxonomy, Challenges, and Open Questions.** Lei Huang et al. 2023. arXiv:2311.05232; ACM Transactions on Information Systems, DOI 10.1145/3703155. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05232 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 19 Nov 2024; comments say accepted at TOIS). Claim: taxonomy of LLM hallucination and its causes, survey of detection methods, benchmarks and mitigation, and the limits of retrieval augmentation as a fix.
- **Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation.** Ziwei Ji et al. 2023. arXiv:2202.03629; ACM Computing Surveys, DOI 10.1145/3571730. https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03629 . Verified: arXiv (v7, 14 Jul 2024; journal-ref on page). Claim: the earlier, task-organised survey (summarisation, dialogue, QA, data-to-text, MT) of hallucination metrics and mitigation; cite for the general definition, Huang et al. for the LLM-era taxonomy.
- **Program of Thoughts Prompting: Disentangling Computation from Reasoning for Numerical Reasoning Tasks.** Wenhu Chen, Xueguang Ma, Xinyi Wang, William W. Cohen. 2022. arXiv:2211.12588; TMLR 2023 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12588 . Verified: arXiv (v4, 23 Oct 2023). Claim: have the LM write a program and an external interpreter compute the answer; about 12% average gain over chain-of-thought across five math datasets and three financial QA datasets (FinQA, ConvFinQA, TATQA), few-shot and zero-shot. This is the direct precedent for "figures come from code, not from the model".
- **FinQA: A Dataset of Numerical Reasoning over Financial Data.** Zhiyu Chen et al. 2021. arXiv:2109.00122; EMNLP 2021 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00122 . Verified: arXiv (v3, 7 May 2022). Claim: expert-written QA pairs over financial reports annotated with gold reasoning programs; large pre-trained models fall far short of expert humans (no number in the abstract).
- **TAT-QA: A Question Answering Benchmark on a Hybrid of Tabular and Textual Content in Finance.** Fengbin Zhu et al. 2021. arXiv:2105.07624; ACL 2021 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07624 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 1 Jun 2021). Claim: hybrid table+text finance QA needing addition, subtraction, comparison, counting; the TAGOP baseline reaches 58.0% F1 against 90.8% expert human F1.

#### Verification, critique and self-correction

- **Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning.** Noah Shinn et al. 2023. arXiv:2303.11366; NeurIPS 2023 main track. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366 and https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/1b44b878bb782e6954cd888628510e90-Abstract.html . Verified: arXiv (v4, 10 Oct 2023) and NeurIPS proceedings page. Claim: agents reflect verbally on task feedback and keep the reflections in an episodic memory buffer; 91% pass@1 on HumanEval vs 80% for GPT-4. Note: the NeurIPS author list omits Edward Berman, who is on the arXiv version.
- **CRITIC: Large Language Models Can Self-Correct with Tool-Interactive Critiquing.** Zhibin Gou et al. 2023. arXiv:2305.11738; ICLR 2024 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738 . Verified: arXiv (v4, 21 Feb 2024). Claim: the model validates and revises its own output by interacting with external tools (search, interpreter); consistent gains on free-form QA, math program synthesis and toxicity reduction; the abstract stresses that external feedback is what makes self-improvement work.
- **Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection.** Akari Asai et al. 2023. arXiv:2310.11511. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511 . Verified: arXiv (v1, 17 Oct 2023; no venue on page). Claim: one LM learns to retrieve on demand and critique its own output with reflection tokens; 7B and 13B models outperform ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama2-chat on open-domain QA, reasoning and fact verification, with better factuality and citation accuracy in long-form output.
- **Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet.** Jie Huang et al. 2023. arXiv:2310.01798; ICLR 2024 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 14 Mar 2024). Claim: intrinsic self-correction (no external feedback) does not improve reasoning and sometimes degrades it; the counterweight to Reflexion/Self-Refine and the argument for grounding verification in tools or data.
- **Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models.** Shehzaad Dhuliawala et al. 2023. arXiv:2309.11495. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 25 Sep 2023; no venue on page). Claim: draft, plan verification questions, answer them independently so the draft does not bias them, then produce a verified response; reduces hallucination on Wikidata list questions, closed-book MultiSpanQA and long-form generation (no number in the abstract).

#### Memory, context and compaction

- **Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts.** Nelson F. Liu et al. 2023/2024. arXiv:2307.03172; TACL 12:157-173 (2024), DOI 10.1162/tacl_a_00638. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172 and https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/ . Verified: arXiv (v3, 20 Nov 2023) and ACL Anthology. Claim: on multi-document QA and key-value retrieval, performance is highest when the relevant passage is at the start or end of the context and degrades significantly when it sits in the middle, including for explicitly long-context models.
- **MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems.** Charles Packer et al. 2023. arXiv:2310.08560. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 12 Feb 2024; no venue on page). Claim: virtual context management moves data between fast and slow memory tiers under interrupt-driven control flow, evaluated on long-document analysis and multi-session chat (no number in the abstract).
- **The Complexity Trap: Simple Observation Masking Is as Efficient as LLM Summarization for Agent Context Management.** Tobias Lindenbauer, Igor Slinko, Ludwig Felder, Egor Bogomolov, Yaroslav Zharov. 2025. arXiv:2508.21433; NeurIPS 2025 DL4C workshop (camera-ready per v3 comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433 . Verified: arXiv (v3, 27 Oct 2025). Claim: on SWE-bench Verified with SWE-agent across five model configurations, masking older environment observations roughly halves cost relative to the raw agent while matching or slightly exceeding the solve rate of LLM summarisation; a hybrid cuts cost a further 7% vs masking and 11% vs summarisation; v3 adds an OpenHands generality probe. (The abstract gives no solve-rate percentages.)

#### Prompt injection and agent security

#### Durable execution, event sourcing, exactly-once

- **Event Sourcing.** Martin Fowler. 12 December 2005. https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html . Verified: page. Claim: the one-line definition, "Capture all changes to an application state as a sequence of events."
- **Sagas.** Hector Garcia-Molina, Kenneth Salem. 1987. Proc. ACM SIGMOD 1987, pp. 249-259, DOI 10.1145/38713.38742. https://doi.org/10.1145/38713.38742 . - **Idempotence Is Not a Medical Condition.** Pat Helland. 2012. Communications of the ACM 55(5):56-65, May 2012, DOI 10.1145/2160718.2160734. https://doi.org/10.1145/2160718.2160734 . The ACM Queue version (DOI 10.1145/2181796.2187821) is UNVERIFIED (403/429 on both ACM Queue and Crossref).
- **State Management in Apache Flink: Consistent Stateful Distributed Stream Processing.** Paris Carbone et al. 2017. The academic anchor for a replay-based durable orchestrator.

### Inferences
- The "tool-selection accuracy falls as the tool count grows" claim should be attributed to MCP-Zero (Fei et al. 2025, Table 1: 97.60 to 69.23 single-turn and 100.00 to 60.22 multi-turn on Claude-3.5) with RAG-MCP (Gan and Sun 2025, 13.62% baseline vs 43.13%) as the second source; neither paper's abstract states a per-tool-count curve, and MCP-Zero's drop is model-dependent.
- No single academic paper defines "exactly-once delivery"; the defensible citation pair is Helland 2012 (at-least-once plus idempotence) and Carbone et al. 2017 (consistent snapshots in Flink), with Durable Functions 2021 for replay-based durable execution.
- For harness surveys the safest citations are Guo et al. 2026 (arXiv, six runtime responsibilities) and Meng et al. 2026 (Preprints.org DOI); the Li et al. ETCLOVG survey has no stable id yet.

### Cited Findings

- **SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?** Carlos E. Jimenez et al. 2023. arXiv:2310.06770; ICLR 2024 (per arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770 . Verified: arXiv (v3, 11 Nov 2024). Claim: 2,294 issues from 12 Python repositories; the best model at publication (Claude 2) resolved 1.96%.
- **Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback.** Aman Madaan et al. 2023. arXiv:2303.17651. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 25 May 2023; no venue on page). Claim: one LM generates, critiques and refines its own output with no extra training; about 20% absolute average improvement over one-step generation on 7 tasks with GPT-3.5, ChatGPT and GPT-4. - **Not what you've signed up for: Compromising Real-World LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt Injection.** Kai Greshake et al. 2023. arXiv:2302.12173. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12173 . Verified: arXiv (v2, 5 May 2023; no venue on page). Claim: the paper that named indirect prompt injection: retrieved data can carry instructions because LLM applications blur data and instructions; taxonomy of impacts (data theft, worming, ecosystem contamination) with demonstrations against Bing Chat and code-completion engines.

### Inferences
- For the security section the natural triple is Greshake 2023 (threat), AgentDojo 2024 (measurement) and CaMeL 2025 (by-design defence), with the Instruction Hierarchy as the model-side mitigation.
- For judge reliability, MT-Bench's "over 80% agreement" should be cited together with Thakur et al.'s finding that only the largest judges align and percent agreement hides 5-point score gaps.
